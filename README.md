Lewy Body Dementia — Focus on attention, visual-spatial processing, working memory, and reaction consistency. A game could use simple visual patterns, spatial matching, and attention tasks.
A paper on this topic usually flows in a simple order: an introduction to Lewy body dementia and why early screening matters, then background on the disease (alpha-synuclein and Lewy bodies, how it differs from Alzheimer's, the core features such as fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations, REM sleep behavior disorder and parkinsonism). After that comes the cognitive profile (attention, executive function and visuospatial deficits with relatively preserved early memory), then the game's design, where each of the four tasks is tied to a domain and the reasoning is explained. It ends with limitations, such as the arbitrary score cutoffs and the fact that a terminal game can only screen, not diagnose, and a short conclusion. For sources, search Google Scholar or PubMed for the 2017 consensus criteria from the DLB Consortium (McKeith et al., published in *Neurology*), plus review articles on cognitive fluctuations and visuospatial impairment in dementia with Lewy bodies. The Lewy Body Dementia Association and the Alzheimer's Association also have readable overviews.
